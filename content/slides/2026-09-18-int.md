{{< speaker_note >}}
*Hello*, I'm Laurent Perrinet from the Institute, and during this talk, I will be speaking about modelling memory, that is about using models of neural networks storing patterns with duration of the order of seconds. This is a hard problem as spiking neurons have a limited memory of the order of tens of milliseconds. How can one extend this memory to larger durations? Here, I will be presenting a method for building *WM in Spiking Neural Networks by using chains of polychronous groups using Heterogeneous Delays*. 

These slides are available from my web-site, along with a number of references.

But first, why is memory important?  Why Modeling Memory?

Let's start with a philosophical insight

We build an implementation of the network using snnTorch - and the delays add just another level of propagation in the unrolled computational graph - here represented by the delay line on the bottom. implementing a 512 neurons network with 41 delays and 8 different patterns

we define the task as repeating *exactly* all spikes from a randomly drawn target with firing probability 1 spike per second. the loss will be the F1-score, that is the harmonic mean between recall and precision. using a fastsigmoid surrogate gradient approximation, the networks learns the target in approximately 10 minutes on a laptop

## Methods : Weight initialization

{{% fragment %}} 
$$ I_j(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \bigl ( \sum_{d=1}^{D} \mathbf{W}_{j, i, d} \cdot s_i(t-d) \bigr ) $$
$$ u_j(t) = \beta \cdot u_j(t-1) \cdot (1 - s_j(t-1)) + I_j(t) $$
$$ s_j(t) = \mathbf{H}[u_j(t) \geq \vartheta] $$
{{% /fragment %}}
{{% fragment %}} 
- $$ \mathbf{W} \mathbf{C} \approx \mathbf{S} $$
{{% /fragment %}}
{{% fragment %}} 
$$ w_{j, i, d} = \frac{1}{N \cdot D \cdot p_A \cdot M} \sum_{\mu=1}^{M} \sum_{t=D+1}^{T} s_{j}^{\mu}(t) \cdot s_i^{\mu}(t-d) $$
{{% /fragment %}}

{{< speaker_note >}}

However, convergence is quite slow, in particular because some places in the weight space may correspond to non-linear (dead or epileptic) regimes.

one may however use a weight initiaialization. indeed each prediction can be seen as a linear prediction of the next time step, and one may concatenate alla theses equations together and then invert it to get the weight using a moore penrose pseudo inverse.

note that since - hence the reason why hebbian-like learning may incidentally work for training such type of networks
