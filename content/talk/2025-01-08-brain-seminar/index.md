abstract: The retinas of many animals, including humans, have a specialized area of
  high visual acuity called the fovea, while peripheral regions have lower resolution.
  This spatial organization, known as retinotopy, is important for biological vision,
  but is largely absent in machine learning. In our study, we investigate how retinotopy
  affects the performance of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in image classification.
  By adding a retinotopic transformation to the input layer of standard ResNet models
  and retraining them, we achieve similar classification accuracy while improving
  the network's ability to handle zooms and rotations. The trade-off for this invariance
  is increased sensitivity to fixation point shifts. Consequently, the output probability
  at different gaze positions can be used as a cue to identify objects of interest.
  Overall, this study suggests why retinotopic mapping in foveated vision implements
  prior knowledge about visual object geometry, providing an efficient solution to
  the visual search problem, an essential function for predators.
