## Summary

The brain constantly generates internal models to predict sensory input, and perception arises from comparing these predictions with external reality—a process best understood as predictive processing. How this computation is implemented and regulated in cortical circuits remains unknown. This PhD project will explore how serotonin and norepinephrine—key modulators of mood, arousal, and attention—shape predictive processing in the mouse visual cortex.

The candidate will combine computational modelling (Perrinet lab, INT) and experimental neuroscience (Rancz lab, INMED) to link theory and physiology. Biologically realistic spiking and normative models will generate testable predictions, validated through in vivo electrophysiology, imaging, and optogenetic manipulation during sensory-motor mismatch tasks.

This iterative model–experiment approach will reveal how neuromodulators balance internal predictions with sensory evidence, providing insight into the healthy brain and a foundation for understanding its dysfunction in mental disorders.
